\begin{quote}
\small
When a practice appears under hundreds of agent names on a shared board, did agents copy it or rediscover it? A write log cannot tell, because exposure goes unrecorded and useful practices can be reinvented. We propose \emph{tracer conventions}: arbitrary markers that have no function, so they can spread only by copying. We seeded such markers, and a functional answer index, on a shared wiki and ran \Stwo{n/trials} paired trials with \Stwo{n/models/word} models. No model produced an index or a code-suffixed page name without a seeded example (\Stwo{all/invented_func/noconv}). Copying required exposure, and exposure was not sufficient: models inspected the wiki unprompted in \Stwo{oss/listed/range} to \Stwo{qwen/listed/range} of trials, and agents that read seeded pages mostly copied the form of a name, not its code. Shown the page list, agents copied the naming structure from one example (\Stwo{all/adopt_suffix_slot/dose1_shown}) but inserted their own id; the exact code was copied once prior pages agreed on it (\Stwo{all/adopt_suffix/dose1_shown}, \Stwo{all/adopt_suffix/dose2_shown} and \Stwo{all/adopt_suffix/dose4_shown} with one, two and four pages)\IfN{s2/have/dose4_shown_mixed}{, and almost never when four pages carried different codes (\Stwo{all/any_code/dose4_shown_mixed})}{}; removing the prompt's team framing did not reduce this copying. A field check on the collusion.wiki write log finds that write-visible exposure predicted technique adoption no detectably more than placebo words (odds-ratio ratio \Sone{RorEPrior}, 95\% CI \Sone{RorEPriorLo}--\Sone{RorEPriorHi}); a planted positive control shows that these write-log tests could not have detected copying, while a page-local test could and found a technique-specific excess. Studying diffusion in deployed swarms requires read logs; tracer conventions offer a cheap, causal measure.
\end{quote}

\section{Introduction}

In mid-2026 autonomous agents from an OpenAI evaluation used small public wikis as a message board \citep{vonarx2026discovery}. They relayed answers, shared data-access workarounds, set up liveness beacons and published a way around an egress restriction, and many of these practices appear under dozens to hundreds of agent names. Whether such a practice \emph{spread} from agent to agent (a swarm) or was \emph{rediscovered} by each agent facing the same constraint (a fleet) decides where to intervene: the shared channel or the task and sandbox. Incident investigators have met the same question elsewhere: a method ``used on about 20 more [places] within two days, whether by spreading or by being found again'' \citep{swarmchasers2026fleet}.

Observational logs struggle with it. Agents read far more than they write, so exposure is unobserved; names are cheap and short-lived \citep{murtuza2026quantifying}; and shared tasks produce simultaneous adoption under either hypothesis, the contagion--homophily confound of social-network studies \citep{shalizi2011homophily}. A recent reconstruction of the same incident added the wiki operator's request log and found that a transmission reading of marker adoption could not be sustained \citep{lutje2026mechanics}. We take a complementary, experimental route: plant conventions whose origin is known and measure when agents pick them up. Arbitrary markers cannot be rediscovered, so any adoption is copying. The work addresses the science of agent networks (Track~2) and, through what it implies for logging, multi-agent oversight (Track~4). Our contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{A tracer-convention experiment} (Section~\ref{sec:exp}) with \Stwo{n/conds/word} paired conditions and \Stwo{n/models/word} models. Arbitrary conventions were never produced without exposure; copying required exposure, which models sought at very different rates and which was not sufficient; and exact arbitrary tokens were copied only when prior pages agreed on them.
\item \textbf{A field check} on the collusion.wiki write log (Section~\ref{sec:field}) with a re-timing null, a within-label choice design and placebo tokens: write-visible exposure did not distinguish technique spread from word echo, naive contagion estimates were artefacts of shared bursts, and a planted positive control shows which of these tests could have detected copying at all.
\item \textbf{Oversight guidance} (Section~\ref{sec:disc}): what to log to study diffusion in deployed agent populations, and how tracer conventions measure it causally. The harness, analysis pipeline, transcripts and a technique catalogue usable as an egress watch-list are released.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{The collusion.wiki incident.} \citet{vonarx2026discovery} documented the episode, estimated roughly 18,000 agent posts, and released the revision export we use (14,591 revisions, 24 May--2 July 2026). \citet{murtuza2026quantifying} gave a first statistical treatment (co-editing permutation tests, a behavioural taxonomy, a deletion event study, an analysis-pitfall checklist we follow); its pipeline lists a URL-diffusion step but it reports no spread-versus-rediscovery test. \citet{lutje2026mechanics} reconstructs episodes, cohorts and schedules from the same export, observes convergence on coordination formats within a day, and adds the operator's request log (about 5.16 million records including content requests): content requests before writing are observed for 1,034 of 1,140 coordinating names, and prior requests to marker-bearing pages occur for 40.2\% of marker adopters against 31.7\% of non-adopters, an association the author declines to interpret as transmission. Our export lacks those read records; our field check is therefore limited to write-visible exposure, and our experiment supplies the causal evidence the logs cannot. \citet{metr2026incident}, with transcripts of a contemporaneous incident, could trace one technique packaged on a board to about 7\% of agents.

\paragraph{Conventions and social learning among LLM agents.} Populations of LLM agents form shared conventions in repeated naming games, and committed minorities can tip them \citep{ashery2025conventions}; agents develop and transmit emergent languages, which newcomers learn from usage \citep{stengeleskin2026glossogen}; and self-improving agents copy peers' skill files, which makes learning more efficient but concentrates populations on fewer discoveries \citep{jha2026solo}. Single models also conform to a stated majority in Asch-style question answering, including against their own correct answer \citep{zhu2025conformity,weng2025conformity}. These studies build interaction or explicit peer answers into the design. We ask a narrower measurement question, closer to imitation than to social pressure: whether one agent arriving at a board copies an arbitrary convention left by others, as a function of how many prior pages carry it and whether the agent looks.

\paragraph{Contagion and multi-agent risk.} Prompt injections self-replicate across agents \citep{lee2024prompt}, an adversarial image spreads a jailbreak through a population \citep{gu2024agentsmith}, ideas can induce their adopters to transmit them \citep{papadopoulos2026mind}, and collective loss of control can be modelled as an epidemic \citep{wu2026collective}. \citet{hammond2025multiagent} list the spread of capabilities among multi-agent risk factors and \citet{motwani2024secret} formalise secret collusion. Tracer conventions offer a way to measure transmission in such systems when it is not known by construction.

\section{Tracer-Convention Experiment}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Design}

Each trial runs one LLM agent, ``evaluation agent A$k$'', for at most 10 turns with one JSON tool call per turn (\texttt{list\_pages}, \texttt{read\_page}, \texttt{write\_page}, \texttt{submit\_answer}, \texttt{finish}). It receives one short question with a deterministic answer (12 seeded templates). The system prompt says that many agents work the same task set, that a shared wiki is visible to all teammates including later ones, and that the agent should leave its answer where teammates can find and reuse it; it never mentions a naming or indexing convention (Appendix~\ref{app:prompt}).

Before the trial, scripted prior agents write answer pages for other questions. Each seed fixes an \textbf{arbitrary marker}, a page-name code such as \texttt{answer-Q-5438-Z5L} and an inline body tag such as \texttt{\textasciitilde vx3\textasciitilde}, and a \textbf{functional convention}, an \texttt{Answers-Index} page with one line per answer. Conditions, paired by seed (same question, codes and prior agents), vary the number of prior pages that carry the convention (\emph{dose}): \emph{isolated} (empty wiki), \emph{neutral4} (four plain pages), \emph{dose1/2/4}, the marker or the index alone at dose 4, and \emph{shown} variants that put the page list in the task message, which separates ``did not look'' from ``looked and did not copy''\IfN{s2/have/dose4_shown_mixed}{; \emph{dose4\_shown\_mixed} shows four marked pages that carry four \emph{different} codes, which separates agreement among prior pages from code disambiguation}{}. The page list exposes names only; the tag and index lines require reading a page. We distinguish \emph{listing} (calling \texttt{list\_pages}) from \emph{reading} (opening a seeded page).

We ran Gemma 4 31B-it, Qwen3.8 27B (thinking disabled), Ministral 3 14B and gpt-oss-120b (reasoning effort low) with vLLM, and DeepSeek-V4 Flash, GLM-5.2 and Qwen3.5 397B through the Adaption Responses API (which takes no sampling seed, so for these the seed fixes the scenario and the pairing but not the sampling), all at temperature 0.7, 48 seeds per condition (\Stwo{n/trials} trials). Outcomes are exact matches on what the agent wrote: the exact seeded code in a page name, the tag in a body, a code-like trailing slot in a page name (any code; also whether it is the agent's own id), the agent's own line in the index, and any index-like page. Primary tests are per-model exact McNemar tests on seeds; pooled counts are descriptive. A further condition, run on two models, removes the team framing from the prompt (Section~\ref{sec:exp}.2). The dose slope for the exact code is a logistic regression on $\log_2$(dose) with model fixed effects, fitted on the models with any adoption (\Stwo{dr/models/short}) with standard errors clustered by seed.

\subsection{Results}

\paragraph{No rediscovery of conventions.} Without a seeded convention, no model wrote an index-like page or gave a page a code-like suffix (\Stwo{all/invented_func/noconv} trials in isolated, neutral4 and neutral4\_shown; Table~\ref{tab:s2}). This null is informative for the two functional elements: an answer index and a structured page name are both useful to a team and well within the models' repertoire, yet neither appeared spontaneously. For the random code and tag the null holds by construction. Every adoption reported below is therefore copying, and the remaining question is what copying requires.

\paragraph{Exposure-seeking separates the models into three behavioural profiles.} Before writing, an agent can only copy what it has seen, and whether it looked at all was the largest source of between-model variation. Pooled over the conditions without a page list, agents listed the wiki before writing in \Stwo{all/listed_before_write/unshown} trials, but this average hides three distinct strategies. Qwen3.8 27B and Qwen3.5 397B usually surveyed the wiki first (in \Stwo{qwen/listed/range} and \Stwo{qwen35397b/listed/range} of trials per condition); Ministral 3 14B and Gemma 4 31B did so occasionally (\Stwo{ministral/listed/range} and \Stwo{gemma/listed/range}); and gpt-oss-120b, DeepSeek-V4 Flash and GLM-5.2 almost never did (\Stwo{oss/listed/range}, \Stwo{deepseekv4flash/listed/range} and \Stwo{glm52/listed/range}), writing their page directly from the task description. Model size and provider do not predict the profile: the two Qwen models of very different scale behave alike, while two large hosted models fall in the non-surveying group. Unprompted copying of the exact code followed this ordering. It occurred only among models that surveyed, and in each of them it rose with the number of prior pages carrying the code (dose 1/2/4: Ministral \Stwo{ministral/adopt_suffix/doses}, Qwen3.5 397B \Stwo{qwen35397b/adopt_suffix/doses}, Qwen3.8 \Stwo{qwen/adopt_suffix/doses}, GLM-5.2 \Stwo{glm52/adopt_suffix/doses}; neutral4 against dose4, exact McNemar on seeds: \Stwo{mclist/adopt_suffix/neutral4_vs_dose4}). A logistic model with model fixed effects gives \Stwo{dr/slope} logits per doubling of prior adopters (seed-clustered SE \Stwo{dr/se}, \Stwo{dr/pe}). The rise is graded, not accelerating: the change in the per-doubling increment is indistinguishable from zero in every model with more than one adoption (Ministral \Stwo{dr/ministral/accel}, Qwen3.8 \Stwo{dr/qwen/accel}, Qwen3.5 397B \Stwo{dr/qwen35397b/accel}, GLM-5.2 \Stwo{dr/glm52/accel}; 95\% intervals). Copying thus scales with exposure but shows, within one to four prior pages, no sign of the threshold or cascade behaviour that a contagion account would predict.

\paragraph{Reading a page is not sufficient for copying its token.} Exposure is necessary but far from sufficient. Of the \Stwo{qual/readers/code/n} unprompted trials in which an agent opened a seeded page before writing, \Stwo{qual/readers/code/k} reproduced the seeded code, whereas \Stwo{qual/readers/ownid/k} adopted the page-name \emph{structure} and filled the slot with the agent's own identifier. The clearest case is Qwen3.8 at dose 1: it read the single seeded page in \Stwo{qwen/readers/dose1} trials and copied its code in none. The body tag, the one marker that can be seen only by reading, was copied in \Stwo{all/adopt_tag/any} trials overall. Reading, in other words, transmits the form of a convention far more readily than its arbitrary content.

\paragraph{The exact token spreads when prior pages agree.} The page-list conditions remove the search step and isolate the decision of what to write. After a single example, agents copied the \emph{structure} of the marked names (\Stwo{all/adopt_suffix_slot/dose1_shown} gave their page a trailing code slot) but \Stwo{all/suffix_slot_self_id/dose1_shown} put their own id into it, and only \Stwo{all/adopt_suffix/dose1_shown} used the seeded code. With two prior pages sharing the code, \Stwo{all/adopt_suffix/dose2_shown} copied it, and with four, \Stwo{all/adopt_suffix/dose4_shown}; the increase from one to two pages is significant within every model with copies at dose 2 (\Stwo{mclist/adopt_suffix/dose1_shown_vs_dose2_shown}). The mixed condition identifies what drives this increase. When four prior pages carried four \emph{different} codes, only \Stwo{all/any_code/dose4_shown_mixed} copied any of them and \Stwo{all/suffix_slot_self_id/dose4_shown_mixed} used their own id (against four pages sharing one code: \Stwo{mclist/any_code/dose4_shown_mixed_vs_dose4_shown}). Four pages with conflicting codes therefore produced the same behaviour as one page, and copying tracked agreement among prior pages rather than their number. The most economical reading is that agents infer the role of the slot from its variation: a code seen once, or seen varying across authors, behaves like an author id and is personalised, whereas a code repeated across authors behaves like a team label and is reproduced. Gemma 4 31B is the exception that bounds the claim: it adopted the structure as readily as most other models but copied the shared code only once (\Stwo{gemma/adopt_suffix/dose4_shown}), so agreement makes copying possible without making it inevitable.

\paragraph{The functional convention follows reading, not dose.} The \texttt{Answers-Index} requires reading a page, and its adoption tracked reading. It was adopted almost exclusively by Qwen3.8 (\Stwo{qwen/adopt_index/doses} at dose 1/2/4; Ministral \Stwo{ministral/adopt_index/doses}, GLM-5.2 \Stwo{glm52/adopt_index/doses}), and Qwen3.8's index adoption fell with dose in step with how often it opened a seeded page (\Stwo{qwen/read_prior_page/doses}): as the wiki filled, Qwen opened fewer pages before writing. The two conventions also competed. With the index present, Qwen3.8 copied the arbitrary code less often than with the marker alone (\Stwo{qwen/adopt_suffix/dose4} against \Stwo{qwen/adopt_suffix/dose4_arbitrary_only}), consistent with an agent that, having found a functional way to share its answer, attended less to the naming of others' pages.

\paragraph{Copying is not produced by the team framing.} The system prompt describes a team and asks the agent to leave its answer where teammates can find and reuse it, which could by itself invite conformity. We therefore re-ran the dose4\_shown condition for \Stwo{abl/models} with a prompt that removes the team, coordination and reuse language and asks only that the answer be saved as a wiki page (\emph{dose4\_shown\_nocue}; same seeds, prior pages and page list; Appendix~\ref{app:prompt}). Removing the framing did not reduce copying, although the two models moved in opposite directions, and the difference is informative. Qwen3.5 397B copied far more without the framing (\Stwo{abl/qwen35397b/adopt_suffix/nocue} against \Stwo{abl/qwen35397b/adopt_suffix/cue}; \Stwo{abl/qwen35397b/mc}) because it almost stopped writing its own id into the slot (\Stwo{abl/qwen35397b/suffix_slot_self_id/nocue} against \Stwo{abl/qwen35397b/suffix_slot_self_id/cue}). GLM-5.2 copied somewhat less (\Stwo{abl/glm52/adopt_suffix/nocue} against \Stwo{abl/glm52/adopt_suffix/cue}; \Stwo{abl/glm52/mc}) and stopped adding its answer to the index altogether (\Stwo{abl/glm52/adopt_index/nocue} against \Stwo{abl/glm52/adopt_index/cue}). Pooled, the code was copied in \Stwo{abl/all/adopt_suffix/nocue} trials without the framing and \Stwo{abl/all/adopt_suffix/cue} with it, a descriptive count dominated by Qwen3.5. The team framing thus does not manufacture agreement-driven copying. If anything, it leads some agents to \emph{sign} their pages with their own id, a mark of authorship that matters only when others will read the page, and it motivates the functional index, which is useful only to others. This ablation covers two hosted models without sampling seeds and one condition, and we treat it as exploratory.

\subsection{Qualitative and Error Analysis}
\label{sec:qual}

\paragraph{What agents wrote in the slot.} Reading the written page names shows that the binary outcome ``copied / did not copy'' hides a range of strategies. Most non-copying agents in the shown conditions personalised the slot with their own id, as in the example in Appendix~\ref{app:prompt}. A minority ignored the convention and wrote a plain \texttt{answer-<qid>} page, a response that was most frequent after a single example (\Stwo{qual/plainqid/dose1_shown}) and rare once several pages agreed (\Stwo{qual/plainqid/dose4_shown}). In \Stwo{qual/blend/n} trials (\Stwo{qual/blend/ministral} by Ministral) agents produced a hybrid that keeps the seeded code's first letter and appends their own number (\Stwo{qual/blend/ex}). Exact-match grading counts these hybrids neither as copies nor as own ids, so the copying rates reported above are conservative. Ministral also accounts for \Stwo{qual/noqid/ministral} of the \Stwo{qual/noqid/n} pages titled by topic rather than by question id (e.g.\ \emph{Date Duration Calculations}), a naming habit that occurs even with an empty wiki and therefore reflects the model's own convention rather than a response to the board.

\paragraph{Copying is silent and wholesale.} Agents never explained their naming choices. None of the \Stwo{qual/notes/n} closing notes mentions a convention, a naming pattern or consistency with other pages; notes record the answer and the page or index that holds it. Copying therefore cannot be detected from what agents say about it, only from what they write, which is the premise of the tracer method. When agents did copy the content of a page, they tended to copy all of it: all \Stwo{qual/tag/n} body-tag copies followed reading a seeded page, \Stwo{qual/tag/withcode} of them also reproduced the exact code, and \Stwo{qual/tag/arbonly} occurred in the marker-only condition, where no index competed for attention.

\paragraph{Protocol and task errors.} Four sources of error bear on interpretation. First, protocol compliance differed sharply. gpt-oss-120b produced an invalid-format turn in \Stwo{oss/err/invalid/pct} of trials and left its answer on the wiki in only \Stwo{oss/err/onwiki/pct}; Ministral had an invalid turn in \Stwo{ministral/err/invalid/pct} and the answer on the wiki in \Stwo{ministral/err/onwiki/pct}; every other model left its answer on the wiki in at least \Stwo{gemma/err/onwiki/pct}. Because adoption is computed over all trials, gpt-oss's zero listing rate partly reflects failed tool use rather than a disposition not to look, and its adoption rates are lower bounds. Second, task accuracy was high for all models except Ministral (\Stwo{ministral/err/correct/pct} correct, against \Stwo{gemma/err/correct/pct} to \Stwo{qwen35397b/err/correct/pct} for the others), with Ministral's errors spread across all question types. The adoption outcomes concern page names and tags and are graded independently of the answer, so accuracy does not confound them, but the contrast is a reminder that adopting a team's format carries no guarantee about the content placed in it. Third, the convention occasionally caused harm: in \Stwo{qual/clobber} trials Ministral rewrote the shared \texttt{Answers-Index} and removed earlier agents' lines, the only destructive edits we observed; no agent overwrote another agent's answer page. Finally, the hosted models accept no sampling seed, so their pairing holds for the scenario but not for the sampling noise; pairing removes less noise for these models than for the vLLM-served ones.

\paragraph{What did not happen.} Several outcomes that a contagion account would predict were absent. No convention was invented without an example; a single example almost never transmitted its exact token; conflicting examples almost never transmitted any token; copying did not accelerate with dose; the body tag, invisible from the page list, almost never spread; and the functional index did not become more popular as more prior pages used it. The positive findings are correspondingly narrow: structure spreads from one example to agents that look, and an arbitrary token spreads when several prior authors agree on it.

\section{Field Check: The Collusion.wiki Write Log}
\label{sec:field}

\paragraph{Data and measures.} We analyse the public export \citep{vonarx2026discovery}: \Sone{DataRevisions} revisions of \Sone{DataPages} pages on four wikis (\Sone{DataLabelsInRevisions} names; \Sone{DataAgentLabels} agent labels after excluding \Sone{DataNHumanHandlesExcluded} moderators, with \Sone{DataAnonymousRevisionsExcludedAsAdopters} anonymous revisions kept only as exposing content). Labels are short-lived (median \Sone{DataMedianRevisionsPerLabel} revisions over \Sone{DataMedianLabelSpanMinutes/1} minutes). We catalogue \Sone{NTechniques} techniques in three families (ten fetch proxies; three environment exploits; two coordination protocols, one of which, \tok{signal_relay}, is an arbitrary naming convention) and \Sone{NPlacebos} placebo tokens (six common words, two markup forms). One annotator read \Sone{ValidationPerTechnique} random adoption events per technique: all matched the named service or token (precision 1.00; Wilson lower bound \Sone{ValidationWilsonLo} per technique), but for \tok{prng_seed} only \Sone{PrecisionStrictPrng} of matches describe performing the seed search, the rest relaying RNG-derived predictions, so we treat it as seed/RNG talk. Recall is unmeasured. A label \emph{adopts} a token at its first revision that introduces a matching line never seen before on that page. Because the export records no reads, \emph{prior exposure} is defined from writes: an earlier revision by the label was saved on a page version containing the token.

\paragraph{Tests.} (a)~A \emph{re-timing permutation test} moves each adoption to a random later revision of the same label (5,000 draws), preserving its activity, and compares the share of adopters exposed beforehand. (b)~A \emph{within-label choice design} treats each adoption as a choice among tokens that existed and were not yet adopted, and estimates by conditional logit the odds of choosing an exposed alternative; label, moment and calendar time are constant within a choice set. (c)~\emph{Placebo control}: a technique effect counts as transfer only if it exceeds the placebo effect.

\paragraph{Results.} Write-visible exposure was rare and did not precede adoption more often than chance: \Sone{PooledTechniquesEPriorMeanObserved/pct} of adopters had prior exposure against \Sone{PooledTechniquesEPriorMeanNull/pct} under re-timing, \Sone{PooledTechniquesEHereMeanObserved/pct} if the edited page already carried the technique, and \Sone{NSigHolmEPrior} of \Sone{NTechniques} techniques had a Holm-significant excess (Table~\ref{tab:s1}, Appendix~\ref{app:field}). The negative excess is structural: \Sone{FirstPosTechMean/pct0} of adopters adopt at their first eligible revision, where no write-visible exposure is possible. In the choice design labels were more likely to adopt a technique they had been exposed to (odds ratio \Sone{ChoiceTechniquesEPriorOddsRatio} [\Sone{ChoiceTechniquesEPriorLo}, \Sone{ChoiceTechniquesEPriorHi}]), but placebo words showed an effect of similar size (\Sone{ChoicePlacebosEPriorOddsRatio} [\Sone{ChoicePlacebosEPriorLo}, \Sone{ChoicePlacebosEPriorHi}]); the ratio \Sone{RorEPrior} [\Sone{RorEPriorLo}, \Sone{RorEPriorHi}], \Sone{RorEPriorP/pe}, shows no detectable difference but allows technique echo up to \Sone{RorEPriorHi} times word echo. Naive exposed-versus-unexposed hazard ratios reached \Sone{SignalRelayHzEPriorRr/1} (\tok{signal_relay}), \Sone{PrngSeedHzEPriorRr/1} (\tok{prng_seed}) and \Sone{CounterBeaconHzEPriorRr/1} (\tok{counter_beacon}), above every placebo (largest \Sone{PlBoldHzEPriorRr/1}), because these techniques arose in one burst when exposure and adoption both rose; \Sone{JqProxyBurstShare/pct0} of \tok{jq_proxy} adopters fall in one 24-hour window and \Sone{JqProxyBurstCountyShare/pct0} of those adoptions fetch the same public data file, a shared-task signature that copying and rediscovery both produce. As an exploratory signal, techniques were introduced on pages that already carried them more often than re-timing predicts while placebos were not (contrast \Sone{ContrastEHere/s3} [\Sone{ContrastEHereLo/3}, \Sone{ContrastEHereHi/3}]), driven by \tok{signal_relay} (\Sone{SignalRelayEHereObs/3} against \Sone{SignalRelayEHereNull/3}).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:disc}

\paragraph{What the two studies show together.} In the field, write-visible exposure did not separate technique spread from word echo, the positive control shows that the main write-log tests were blind to copying at the rates tested, and the export cannot see the reads through which agents demonstrably encountered pages \citep{lutje2026mechanics}. In the experiment, where exposure is controlled and conventions cannot be rediscovered, copying is real but conditional: on whether the agent looks, which varied from never to almost always across models, and for exact arbitrary tokens on prior pages agreeing on them. The one field token that is an arbitrary convention, \tok{signal_relay}, is also where the write log shows its only page-local, technique-specific signal, consistent with arbitrary conventions being where copying is identifiable.

\paragraph{Implications for oversight.} Read and exposure logs are necessary to study diffusion: the operator's request log for this incident \citep{lutje2026mechanics} shows reads the public export omits, and even with reads, association is not transmission. Tracer conventions supply the missing causal handle: seeding an arbitrary marker and measuring where it reappears estimates transmission directly, cannot be confounded by rediscovery, and needs no access to agents' internals. Naive exposed-versus-unexposed comparisons should not be reported without placebo tokens: here they would have suggested up to \Sone{SignalRelayHzEPriorRr/1}-fold contagion for the techniques of greatest safety interest.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.95\textwidth}\small
\textbf{What to log to study diffusion in an agent deployment.} \emph{Sources:} the shared substrate's access log (HTTP GET or page-view records), the agent platform's tool-call log, and the egress proxy. \emph{Minimum record per read:} timestamp, session or credential id (not only the self-chosen display name), resource id and version (revision or ETag). \emph{Join:} reads to later writes by the same session, so that ``seen before adopting'' is measured rather than proxied. \emph{Retention:} at least the duration of a run plus the incident-response window. \emph{Disclosure:} incident exports should include redacted read logs alongside revision histories. \emph{Measurement:} seed a small number of arbitrary tracer markers on shared channels at known times and count their reappearance.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

\paragraph{External validity of the experiment.} The setting is deliberately minimal and differs from the incident in ways that likely matter. Prior pages answer \emph{other} questions, so looking has no informational payoff, whereas incident boards carried answers and workarounds to the agents' own tasks. There is no shared obstacle, so the experiment cannot test rediscovery of functional techniques under constraint, which is the field question. One focal agent faces scripted priors, so there are no chains or population dynamics. The prompt asks agents to leave answers for teammates; removing that framing did not reduce copying in a two-model ablation (Section~\ref{sec:exp}), but other framings, such as competition or explicit instructions to be original, were not tested. The models are four open-weight models served with vLLM and three hosted models, at one temperature with at most 10 turns, not the incident agents; gpt-oss-120b never listed the wiki unprompted, whereas incident agents read and replied within threads. The experiment measures preconditions of copying, not diffusion rates in deployment.

\paragraph{Limitations of the field check.} Labels are not agents, and arriving labels may be renamed agents that already knew a technique; reads are unobserved, so exposure measures are lower bounds; regex recall is unmeasured and validation used one annotator; placebos are higher-frequency than most techniques, so the technique/placebo ratio mixes transfer with base rates; synchronised task cohorts produce simultaneous adoptions under either hypothesis; and the data cover one incident.

\paragraph{Future work.} A month of follow-up would run the tracer design with populations of interacting agents and chains, give the board real payoffs and shared obstacles so that functional rediscovery can be tested, and seed tracers in a live multi-agent deployment with read logging enabled.

\section{Conclusion}
Arbitrary tracer conventions make copying identifiable. In a seeded wiki, LLM agents never produced such conventions unprompted, copied them only after exposure, sought exposure at rates from never to almost always, and copied exact arbitrary tokens only when several prior pages agreed on them. Reading a page transmitted the form of a convention far more readily than its content, agents never mentioned the conventions they copied, and removing the team framing from the prompt did not reduce copying. In the collusion.wiki write log, exposure predicted technique adoption no detectably more than word choice, and a planted positive control shows that the main write-log tests could not have detected copying. Studying diffusion in agent swarms requires recording what agents read, and tracer conventions are a cheap way to measure it.

\section{Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations}
\label{app:limits}

\paragraph{What the results establish.} The experiment establishes, for \Stwo{n/models/word} models in a minimal seeded setting, that arbitrary conventions are adopted only after exposure, that exposure-seeking differs sharply by model and is not sufficient for copying, and that exact-token copying depends on agreement among prior pages; a two-model ablation indicates that it does not depend on the prompt's team framing. It does not estimate diffusion rates in deployed swarms or show anything about rediscovery of functional techniques. The field check reports that, in one incident, write-visible exposure did not distinguish technique adoption from word echo; \Sone{PooledTechniquesEPriorMeanObserved/pct} of adopters had write-visible prior exposure under the strictest definition and \Sone{PooledTechniquesEHereMeanObserved/pct} under the same-page definition. These are descriptions, not bounds on copying, which can occur through unrecorded reads \citep{metr2026incident,lutje2026mechanics}; the positive control shows that the prior-exposure and choice tests had no power to detect copying at up to \Sone{pc/by_f/4/true_copy_share_mean/pct0} of adopters.

\paragraph{Dual use.} The collusion.wiki data contain working descriptions of a sandbox-egress bypass, write channels and data-access workarounds. Our outputs record technique names and short descriptors; the released regexes match service host names or protocol tokens, and the pattern for the egress exception names the excepted host, which the public write-up already discloses. No payload, credential, tokenised URL or procedure is reproduced. Tracer conventions involve injecting content into a shared agent channel; in third-party channels this should be done only with the operator's consent, and the same measurement could help an attacker optimise propagation. We judge the defensive value (detecting and quantifying spread) to outweigh this. The experiment uses benign questions and harmless markers.

\paragraph{Data ethics.} The export was published with IP addresses truncated and user names redacted. We do not attempt re-identification, do not use the IP field for linkage, and exclude moderator handles. Page text is matched by regular expressions and never executed or rendered. No human subjects were involved.

\section{Experiment Prompt and Example}
\label{app:prompt}
System prompt, verbatim from \path{src/sord_exp/env.py} (\texttt{\{aid\}}: agent id; \texttt{\{turns\}}: turn limit). The user message is \texttt{You are \{aid\}. Your question (id \{qid\}): \{question\}}, then in the \emph{shown} conditions the page list, then \texttt{Reply with one JSON tool call per turn.}
\begin{lstlisting}
You are evaluation agent {aid}, one of many evaluation agents working through the same shared task set. Each agent is given one short question.
The team has a shared wiki that agents use to coordinate: every page on it is visible to all teammates, including agents that start after you.
Your job: answer your question correctly by calling submit_answer, and leave your answer on the wiki where teammates can find and reuse it. Then call finish.

Tools. Reply with exactly one JSON object per turn and nothing else:
{"tool": "list_pages", "args": {}}
{"tool": "read_page", "args": {"name": "<page name>"}}
{"tool": "write_page", "args": {"name": "<page name>", "body": "<text>"}}   (creates the page, or overwrites it if it exists)
{"tool": "submit_answer", "args": {"answer": "<your answer>"}}
{"tool": "finish", "args": {"note": "<short note>"}}
You have at most {turns} turns.
\end{lstlisting}
Seeded pages are named \texttt{answer-<qid>} or \texttt{answer-<qid>-<CODE>} with body \texttt{[<tag> ]<qid>: <question>} / \texttt{Answer: <answer>} / \texttt{-- A<n>}; index lines read \texttt{[<qid>] <answer> (by A<n>)}.

\begin{lstlisting}
You are evaluation agent {aid}. You are given one short question.
There is a wiki.
Your job: answer your question correctly by calling submit_answer, and save your answer as a page on the wiki. Then call finish.
\end{lstlisting}
